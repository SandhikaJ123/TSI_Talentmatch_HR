🤖 How We Use AI

Our system uses OpenAI's GPT-4 and GPT-4o-mini models at multiple stages of the matching process. Here's exactly where and how AI powers our platform:

1. Candidate Information Extraction

🔍 What AI Does:

AI analyzes the resume text and extracts key information that's often difficult to parse with traditional methods:

  • Full Name: Identifies the candidate's name (avoiding location names or company names)
  • Job Title: Extracts current or desired position (e.g., "Senior Full Stack Developer")
  • Location: Finds city and state information
  • Contact Info: Extracts email and phone number

Model Used: GPT-4o-mini (fast and cost-effective)

Why AI? Resume formats vary wildly. AI can understand context and extract information even from poorly formatted or creative resumes.

2. Strengths & Weaknesses Analysis

💪 What AI Does:

For each candidate, AI generates a comprehensive analysis by comparing the resume against job requirements:

  • Strengths (3-5 points): Specific skills, experience, and achievements that make the candidate a good fit
  • Weaknesses (2-4 points): Gaps in skills or experience relative to job requirements
  • Professional Summary: 2-3 sentence assessment of overall fit

📝 Example AI Analysis

Strengths:

  • Strong MERN stack experience with 5+ years of hands-on development
  • Led team of 5 developers, demonstrating leadership capabilities
  • AWS Certified Solutions Architect with proven cloud expertise
  • Experience with CI/CD pipelines and DevOps practices

Weaknesses:

  • No Docker or Kubernetes experience mentioned
  • Limited exposure to microservices architecture
  • No mention of test-driven development practices

Summary: "Strong candidate with excellent MERN stack skills and leadership experience. AWS certification is a major plus. Would benefit from containerization training to meet all job requirements."

Model Used: GPT-4o-mini

Why AI? AI can understand nuanced context, relate skills to job requirements, and provide human-like professional assessments.

3. Semantic Similarity (Hybrid Mode)

🧠 What AI Does:

In hybrid mode, AI generates embeddings (mathematical representations) of both the job description and resumes:

  • Job Embedding: Converts job requirements into a 1536-dimensional vector
  • Resume Embeddings: Converts each resume into a comparable vector
  • Cosine Similarity: Measures how "close" the resume is to the job requirements in semantic space
Semantic Similarity Formula
similarity = cosine(job_embedding, resume_embedding)
semantic_score = (similarity + 1) / 2 × 100

Model Used: text-embedding-3-small (OpenAI's embedding model)

Why AI? Embeddings capture semantic meaning beyond keyword matching. For example, "React developer" and "Frontend engineer with React.js" are semantically similar even if words differ.

🏆 How Ranking Works

Our ranking system uses a weighted scoring formula that evaluates candidates across four key dimensions. Here's the complete breakdown:

The Master Formula

Final Score Calculation
Final Score = (Skills × 40%) + (Experience × 25%) + (Education × 20%) + (Overall Fit × 15%)
💡 Why These Weights?

Skills are weighted highest (40%) because they're the most direct indicator of job performance. Experience (25%) and Education (20%) are important but secondary. Overall Fit (15%) captures semantic relevance and keyword density.

Note: These weights are customizable! Hiring managers can adjust them based on role requirements.

Component 1: Skills Score (40% weight)

🎯 How It's Calculated

Skills are the most critical factor. We use a sophisticated matching algorithm:

Skills Score Formula
Coverage Score = (Matched Skills / Required Skills) × 100
Depth Score = Average frequency of matched skills × 20
Skills Score = (Coverage × 70%) + (Depth × 30%)

Skill Matching Techniques:

  1. Exact Matching: Word-boundary-aware regex search for each skill
  2. Synonym Detection: Recognizes variations (React = ReactJS = React.js)
  3. Fuzzy Matching: Jaro-Winkler distance algorithm (threshold: 0.92) handles typos
  4. Stemming: Porter Stemmer normalizes words (developing → develop)
  5. Frequency Bonus: Skills mentioned multiple times score higher

📊 Skills Scoring Example

Job Requirements: JavaScript, React, Node.js, AWS, Docker, PostgreSQL (6 skills)

Candidate Resume: JavaScript (mentioned 3×), React (mentioned 5×), Node.js (mentioned 2×), Python (mentioned 1×)

Calculation:

  • Matched Skills: 3 (JavaScript, React, Node.js)
  • Missing Skills: 3 (AWS, Docker, PostgreSQL)
  • Coverage Score: (3 / 6) × 100 = 50%
  • Depth Score: ((3+5+2)/3) × 20 = 66.7%
  • Final Skills Score: (50 × 0.7) + (66.7 × 0.3) = 55%

Component 2: Experience Score (25% weight)

📅 How It's Calculated

Experience is extracted using multiple regex patterns that detect phrases like:

  • "5+ years of experience"
  • "Experience of 7 years"
  • "3 years in software development"
  • "Minimum 5 years required"
Experience Score Formula
If Candidate Years ≥ Required Years:
Score = min(100, 100 + ((Candidate - Required) / Required) × 20)

If Candidate Years < Required Years:
Score = (Candidate Years / Required Years) × 100
